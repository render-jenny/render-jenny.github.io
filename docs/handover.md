# Handover

Newest entry first. Each session adds an entry: what changed, problems (what went wrong, why, how it was fixed), what's next, open questions, and one thing worth learning.

## 2026-11-17 (First version, set up with Foundation)

- **What changed:** built the whole portfolio page in one session and set it up as a Foundation project: `CLAUDE.md`, decisions D001 to D005, a spec, `tests/check.mjs` (23 checks), Foundation's pre-commit and pre-push checks, CI (page checks and a secret scan of the history) and Dependabot for the Actions. Published on 17 November 2026: the repo is public and GitHub Pages serves https://render-jenny.github.io (D002); the owner approved both. Secret scanning, push protection and Dependabot alerts are on. A fresh reviewer (Sonnet, no part in building it) found no blockers; its wording points are under open questions. Before publishing, the Stone Island News video was also checked every 2 seconds (no other publisher's content) and the photos for location data (none).
- **Problems:** (1) An early claim on the Bootcamp Connect card, "installs like an app and works offline", was untrue: it came from the project's README, which describes plans as built. The owner caught it; it was removed, and `CLAUDE.md` now says to describe a project by what it does today. (2) The Stone Island News demo video showed another publisher's whole article page for 17 seconds. That section was cut before the video went into the repo. (3) Setting the GitHub profile's Website field failed: the command-line sign-in lacks the `user` scope, and it was left for the owner to do by hand rather than widen the access. (4) My test screenshots kept showing the sea at the wrong depth, because the page's own resize handler reset the value I forced during testing; the page was right, the test method wasn't.
- **What's next:** (1) add a public contact address to the Contact section once the owner has made her free Proton address (not her private Gmail, which the checks keep off the page); (2) the owner confirms D003, D004 and D005; (3) decide whether the Stone Island News repo goes public, so its card can link to the code (Foundation's checklist first); (4) the owner looks at the page with the keyboard and at 320px wide, which headless checks can't judge; (5) the owner sets the profile Website to the page and pins the repo (GitHub Settings > Public profile); (6) re-record the Bootcamp Connect demo if the app changes.
- **Open questions:** the reviewer's wording points: soften "delivering industry standard products" (first box) to "following industry standards"; remove any skill the owner couldn't discuss in an interview (Next.js, Prisma, SQLite, Gemini API, Docker, Supabase, Sound design, Accessibility); be ready to say which brand and what "led a campaign" meant; check that the Stone Island News card can't read as an official Stone Island product. Also: does the owner want the "industry standard products" phrase in the first box softened to "following industry standards"? Should Next.js, Prisma, SQLite and Gemini API stay in the skills list now that Jobseeker is no longer on the page?
- **One thing worth learning:** a check that can never fail isn't a check. Before trusting `tests/check.mjs`, six faults were planted in a copy of the page, and it caught all six.
